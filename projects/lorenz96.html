Research question

I wanted to know how reliably an Ensemble Kalman filter could recover the state of a chaotic system from sparse, noisy observations. I used the 40-variable Lorenz-96 model (K=40, F=8) and tested how localisation, ensemble size and inflation affect the result.

Methodology

I solved the Lorenz-96 equations with RK4 and estimated the largest Lyapunov exponent using the two-trajectory Benettin method. For state estimation, I used a stochastic Ensemble Kalman filter, Gaspari–Cohn localisation and multiplicative inflation.

Experimental setup and verification

Estimated largest Lyapunov exponent λ₁ ≈ 1.69, consistent with the literature range for K=40, F=8; the EnKF implementation verified against the exact Kalman filter on a linear-Gaussian problem with a known closed-form solution.

Quantitative findings

With 20 ensemble members, Gaspari–Cohn localisation (radius 2) reduces the time-averaged analysis RMSE from 4.63 to 0.41 (10 seeds), below the observation-noise level of 1.0. The unlocalised filter had effectively diverged (its RMSE is barely below the 4.93 of a free forecast, with severe ensemble under-dispersion), so the over-11× ratio measures recovery from filter divergence. I also found that very small ensembles could settle into estimates that looked stable but remained wrong.

Limitations

The localisation radius was chosen from the same 10 seeds that are reported (the next radius tested still gives a 9.5× reduction). Inflation alone is insufficient (best RMSE 3.16). Some observation-density and observation-frequency effects are non-monotonic and not explained. The model parameters are assumed known; I estimated only the state.
